The functional segmental organization of the spinal cord

✔️ Finished project
More details

PI: Julien Doyon, The Neuro, McGill University
Strong collaboration with N. Kinany and D Van de Ville, EPFL, University of Geneva, Switzerland

Using spinal cord functional MRI, we provided the first evidence of robust and reproducible in vivo mapping of functional spinal segments at both the group and individual levels. Building on this work, we demonstrated that resting-state brain–spinal connectivity follows a precise somatotopic organization.

These groundbreaking findings have direct clinical applications... more details soon !

See: Kinany*, Landelle* et al 2024 ; Landelle*, Kinany* et al. 2024

Ultra high field spinal FMRI

[⏳ Ongoing project]
More details

PI: Julien Cohen-Adad, Polytech Montreal
with A. D'Astous, Polytechnique Montreal, Canada


We are optimize functional acquisition and analysis for high-field (7T) spinal fMRI. Imaging at 7T offers higher spatial resolution and contrast, enabling more precise mapping of the spinal function. The enhanced sensitivity of 7T fMRI is expected to reveal laminar specific functional patterns, not detectable at 3T, reflecting the distinct neural circuits at the layer level.

Neural Bases of Body Perception Changes with Aging

[⏳ Ongoing project]
More details

PI: Anne Kavounoudias, Aix-Marseille University, CNRS

We investigated the neural bases of sensorimotor integration in young and older adults. Using proprioceptive 💪🏼 and tactile 🖐🏽 hand-rotation illusions during fMRI, we found a common sensorimotor network across ages but reduced hemispheric lateralization in older adults during proprioceptive stimulation. This imbalance correlated with poorer proprioceptive, but not tactile, perception, suggesting altered interhemispheric coordination with age.

See: Landelle et al 2021 ; Pinzon et al in preparation

Body Perception Changes with Aging

[✔️ Completed project]

More details

This project examined how aging alters sensory integration in perceiving body movement and texture. Using tactile ✋🏻, visual 👁️, auditory 👂🏾, and proprioceptive 💪🏽 stimulations, we found that tactile perception remains stable while muscle proprioception declines. Despite this, older adults show preserved or enhanced multisensory integration, suggesting a compensatory reweighting of sensory inputs.

See: Landelle et al 2018 ; Chancel et al 2018
